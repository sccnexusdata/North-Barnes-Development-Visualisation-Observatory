Current published picture

September 2026 baseline

Homes

up to 3,000

Low-rise neighbourhoods are expected to form the majority of the settlement, with final density and mix subject to detailed design.

Local services

~6,000 m²

Commercial/local-centre space has been reported, including shops and a local supermarket.

Employment

~10,000 m²

Light-industrial/employment space has been described. This should not be represented as major logistics warehousing unless later plans say so.

Community

school + health + sport

Primary/early-years provision, health facilities, sports pitches and pavilion/community provision are among the currently described elements.

Landscape and infrastructure

What should appear in realistic visualisations

Green & blue infrastructure

Retained woodland and hedgerows, green corridors, parkland, nature-recovery areas, sustainable drainage and attenuation ponds should form a substantial part of the visual footprint.

Roads & movement

Principal access and internal distributor streets should be shown only where supported by published transport material. Exact roundabouts and junction geometry remain indicative until detailed drawings are available.

Utilities

Water, wastewater, electricity and telecommunications are necessary, but plant and compound locations must remain marked “to be determined” unless formally published.

Evidence standard

Three confidence levels

Published / evidenced

Directly supported by developer, council, statutory-body or submitted planning material.

Indicative / under assessment

Discussed or necessary, but final location, size or engineering is not yet fixed.

Illustrative reconstruction

A plausible visual representation derived from evidence, never passed off as an official photomontage.

Method

The research workflow uses reproducible geospatial processing so that site boundaries, villages, roads, watercourses, distances and viewpoints remain fixed. Generative imagery is used only as a presentation layer after the real geography has been established.
