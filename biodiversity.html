1 · Baseline habitat

The authority evidence already identifies a connected ecological landscape

Lewes District Council material identifies ecological value around field margins, hedgerows, treelines, woodland blocks, streams, ditches and ponds, with pockets of ancient woodland and nearby Local Wildlife Sites. The Sustainability Appraisal also raises ecological-connectivity and fragmentation concerns around ancient woodland.

Authority-supported habitat context

  • Ancient woodland pockets / network
  • Hedgerows, treelines and field margins
  • Woodland blocks
  • Streams, ditches and ponds
  • Riparian habitat and the Bevern corridor

Exact public geometry remains pending ingestion of the relevant authoritative/licensed spatial layers.

Bevern Stream

Environment Agency water-body ID GB107041012570. The 2022 public classification reports Poor ecological status; fish were Moderate, invertebrates High, and macrophytes/phytobenthos Poor.

This is catchment context. It is not evidence that North Barnes caused the classification.

2 · Species and survey status

Potential is not presence

The council evidence identifies targeted survey need for several protected or ecologically important groups. Until current records and field surveys are loaded, each remains a survey target rather than a confirmed North Barnes occurrence.

Targeted survey groups

  • Bats
  • Dormice
  • Badgers
  • Great crested newts
  • Water voles
  • Invertebrates
  • Breeding birds

Source-supported habitat use

South Downs National Park / Ouse & Adur Rivers Trust material supports use of the Bevern system as spawning habitat for sea trout (Salmo trutta).

This is a corridor/habitat statement, not a precise North Barnes occurrence point.

Record-centre rule: absence of a record is not evidence of absence. Recording effort varies by location and taxonomic group, and some sensitive species records are deliberately generalised or confidential.

Biodiversity Net Gain

Current GOV.UK planning guidance requires the statutory biodiversity-gain objective, where applicable, to deliver at least a 10% increase in biodiversity value relative to the pre-development onsite habitat value.

No North Barnes BNG percentage is published here yet. A credible result requires the submitted baseline habitat survey, condition assessment, statutory metric workbook, development geometry and onsite/offsite assumptions to be reproducible.
